# North Star: LEGO: Twister

## One-Sentence Vision

Build the game that feels like the original *Twister* was adapted during the classic early Traveller's Tales LEGO era: recognizable movie beats, slapstick comedy, chunky construction, constant smashing and rebuilding, collectible-driven exploration, arcade vehicles, secrets, and a tornado that physically rearranges the level around the player.

## Pillars

### Twister first
Every major level, prop family, vehicle, buildable, and spectacle should strengthen the *Twister* fantasy: Wakita, storm chasing, Dorothy-style equipment, farms, roads, barns, utility infrastructure, the drive-in, storm aftermath, rival chasers, escalating tornadoes, the F5 finale, and airborne cows.

### Classic LEGO-game grammar
The core verbs are:

- smash
- collect
- build
- interact
- drive
- discover
- switch abilities/characters
- replay

The world should reward curiosity with studs, gags, secrets, alternate routes, destructible set pieces, unlocks, and Free Play-style replay.

### Tornado as villain
The tornado should move through authored routes, pull and fling props, peel structures in phases, expose and block paths, transform traversal, create rescue moments, threaten vehicles and characters, and produce escalating spectacle.

### Destruction and rebuilding as world language
Scenery should look assembled from pieces worth touching. Large structures should support authored hero-destruction states. Rebuildables should preferably change gameplay by opening routes, repairing equipment, activating machinery, building ramps/bridges, or producing a comedic payoff.

### Slapstick adventure
The tone should stay playful and physical. Storm danger can escalate, but character reactions, prop comedy, ridiculous rebuilds, and LEGO-game elasticity should keep the experience adventurous rather than grim.

### Mobile immediacy
Android/mobile is the priority. Controls, camera, effects, silhouettes, and interaction must stay readable and responsive on a phone. Complexity belongs under the hood.

## Visual North Star

Do not make generic block art or photoreal scenery with studs pasted onto it.

Prefer:

- recognizable LEGO-scale construction logic
- layered plates and readable studs where they matter
- bright plastic materials against stormy skies
- chunky proportions
- authored break seams
- visible construction under storm damage
- small environmental jokes
- 1990s Oklahoma architectural and prop vocabulary

The construction library should directly support clapboard homes, barns, sheds, fences, tractors, storm equipment, roadside signage, utilities, drive-in structures, trucks, trailers, Wakita storefronts, water towers, and damaged variants.

## Construction-Library Test

Every meaningful primitive or module must answer:

> What actual LEGO: Twister level, prop, vehicle, buildable, or destruction state does this help us author?

If the answer is vague, deprioritize it.

## Experience Test

Before accepting a major feature, ask:

> Does this make the player feel more like they are playing a classic LEGO adaptation of *Twister*?

If it could drop unchanged into a generic brick sandbox or generic storm simulator, it needs another design pass.
